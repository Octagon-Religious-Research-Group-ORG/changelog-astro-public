Every ORG chat in one window, a pledge of support, and licenses for ORG's GitHub home

TL;DR: Community now opens all four of ORG's Telegram chats: the public chat, events and ceremonies, and the two private members' groups, with a plain note on how to ask. ORG's own overview document is linked from the About menu and the map. A new page lets anyone sign a pledge of support with a wallet: free, nothing sent, nothing recorded, no counts. 💬 Where ORG gathers, all of it

Community, on every ORG page, used to show one Telegram group and a note to ask Tripp. Tripp OK’d sharing all of ORG’s chats, so the window now shows four:

  • ORG (Public), @ORG_Spirituality: open to anyone, members and anyone curious about ORG. In its own words, it’s “an unrestricted public chat”, so what people say there doesn’t always reflect ORG’s views.
  • ORG Events and Ceremonies: a collaboration between ORG and Entheo Community, where orgspirituality.org’s Community page sends people for news of the monthly meditation ceremony. Same link as before.
  • ORG (Voting), private: for confirmed ORG members, to vote on all of ORG’s decisions.
  • ORG EaMs (Entheo-approved Members), private: for members who have completed an approved form of entheogen safety training.

The two private groups admit people by hand. Tripp approves each request once that person’s membership form (or, for EaMs, their EaM form) is complete, so it’s fine to ask to join now: the request simply waits until then. Each card links ORG’s public membership form. The sneak peek at a network of the members’ own, paired with a DAO, is still at the bottom. ORG’s CONTACTS.md lists the same chats, and Help decide what comes next on Three directions now points at the public chat.

📄 ORG’s own overview

The ORG overview, ORG’s own document about who it is and what it’s building, is now linked as “ORG overview (document)” from the About menu, the footer, / search, the map and ORG’s profile.

🤝 A pledge of support

The new pledge page is a way to say ORG matters to you. You read a short pledge, connect a crypto wallet (a browser wallet, a phone wallet by QR code, or an email sign-in) and sign it:

I support the Octagon Religious-Research Group: a member-governed community where science and spirituality meet, and its work toward octagon temples and research centers where both are practiced side by side.

Signing is a message, not a transaction: no gas, no money, no contract. The signature is checked in your browser and then forgotten; nothing is sent, nothing is recorded, and the page never shows your wallet’s address. There are no counts: a signature proves a wallet signed, not that a unique person did, so no number will appear until pledges can be published as records anyone can check. A small tool below the pledge checks any pledge’s signature in the browser. Signing a pledge doesn’t make you a member; joining ORG is its own step.

🧾 Receipts and taxes

The donate page ends with a short “Receipts and taxes” section: ask ORG for a receipt, and what ORG’s own gifts and contributions page says, quoted: “Donations are tax-deductible”, and “we chose to register as a 501(c)(3)”. How much, if at all, depends on your own situation, and it isn’t tax advice.

⚖️ Licenses

ORG’s GitHub home now has licenses, approved by Christopher: the code is MIT, the written content (these entries, the library, the READMEs) is CC BY-SA 4.0, and ORG’s name, the octagon and its other marks are reserved, as TRADEMARKS.md explains. The three files are in the organization’s profile repository, the entry portal and this AstroLog’s public repository, and the footer of every page says so. The live site’s code and the first site aren’t licensed yet.

  • Donate and the welcome guide are always there: in the footer, the navbar’s More menu and the Menu, on every page.

  • These pages are planning to outlive any one person. The sustainability page and the roadmap now say who holds the keys today (Christopher W), and the steps to hand them to ORG’s own people, including ORG’s own wallet-connect project, one line in its settings.

Shared in Telegram, iMessage or Discord, it arrives with its own preview card.
